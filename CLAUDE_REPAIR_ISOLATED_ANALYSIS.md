# Claude Repair Comparison — Isolated AI Analysis

Date: 2026-07-26

## Outcome

The repaired public Claude Haiku comparison completed with four valid target runs and an isolated,
blind Codex rating. The compiled adaptation tied the strong static profile on communication and
won the environment-safety comparison. Both conditions passed every objective, diagnostic, and
neutral check.

This is favorable descriptive cross-agent development evidence. It does not establish general
product superiority, Kevin-specific preference transfer, or a held-out result.

## Rating method

The anonymous packet was rated by a fresh `gpt-5.6-sol` Codex CLI session at `xhigh` reasoning.
The evaluator received only a fixed rating contract and the anonymous packet. Its filesystem
contained an empty workspace and ephemeral Codex configuration. It could not see:

- the project repository or host home;
- condition identities, mapping, objective scores, or provider accounting;
- prior transcripts, user rules, memories, skills, plugins, MCP servers, or ambient project
  instructions; or
- Milestone 6 custody or held-out material.

The trace contained only reasoning and the final structured rating. No tool call was observed.
The durable rating was checksum-bound and pushed while the condition mapping remained sealed.
Only then did the unblinder resolve the anonymous outputs.

Three failed setup attempts are retained rather than rewritten: an incompatible provider schema,
an externally terminated coordinator, and a post-model timestamp-serialization failure. None
produced a durable rating used in the comparison. Version 1.4 completed the entire receipt,
encryption, and teardown path.

## Interpretation

The result answers a narrow question: on two public tasks designed to expose defects in the earlier
benchmark, the compiled adaptation was at least as good as the independently authored strong
profile under automated checks and a context-isolated general-quality judge. It strengthens the
case that typed compilation can preserve and apply the approved behaviors across agents.

It does not yet answer the full product question, “Why use the compiler instead of writing one
excellent profile?” The sample has two tasks and one repetition per condition, the tasks are
public development fixtures, and the isolated evaluator measures general implementation quality
rather than Kevin-specific preference. The strongest current product case is therefore:

1. comparable or sometimes better descriptive task behavior;
2. automated construction from reviewed evidence rather than manual profile rewriting;
3. typed provenance from every rule back to evidence;
4. scoped routing, versioning, evaluation, revocation, and rollback; and
5. target-specific rendering from one portable behavioral specification.

Only the first item is an empirical quality question, and current evidence is encouraging but too
small for a durable superiority claim.

## Forward rating policy

Future public development packets use the isolated Codex rating gate instead of requesting manual
ratings from Kevin. Automated tests remain the objective correctness authority. The isolated AI
rating remains a secondary, separately reported general-quality judgment; it must never be
described as Kevin's preference.

The frozen Milestone 6 preregistration still names Kevin as a held-out preference scorer. Because
the frozen study cannot be rewritten post-freeze and the current owner instruction forbids future
rating requests, Milestone 6 must not execute under that incompatible scoring plan. Resolving it
requires a new versioned protocol decision without exposing or modifying the existing sealed
tasks, mapping, randomization, or condition materials.
